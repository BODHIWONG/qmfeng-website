A strong founder can build an extraordinary company.

Vision, courage and decisiveness are often exactly what a business needs in its early years.

But there is another side to founder-led success:

**What happens when the company becomes huge, but major decisions still depend heavily on one person's judgement?**

China Evergrande offers a powerful case for reflection.

At its peak, Evergrande was one of China's largest property developers. But its ambitions went far beyond property.

The group expanded into football, tourism, healthcare and electric vehicles — ventures that required enormous amounts of capital and management resources.

The issue is not whether these industries were good or bad.

The more important question is:

**Who could effectively challenge a major decision before billions were committed?**

## Success Can Create Its Own Blind Spot

When a founder has made many successful decisions, confidence grows.

That confidence is often deserved.

But over time, something can quietly change inside the organisation.

People become less willing to disagree. Senior executives learn what the boss wants to hear. Bad news gets softened. Warnings become less direct.

Eventually, management may still have many talented people — but fewer people who can genuinely influence the most important decisions.

This is where risk begins to accumulate.

**The greatest risk is not making a wrong decision. It is building an organisation where no one can effectively challenge the person making it.**

And the more successful the leader has been in the past, the harder this problem can be to recognise.

## Major Decisions Need a Second Perspective

Consider a S$100 million investment.

The financial model may look attractive. The market research may support it. The management team may be confident. The founder may strongly believe in it.

But before committing the capital, there should still be one more question:

**What are we not seeing?**

This question becomes even more important when the person making the decision is emotionally committed to the outcome.

At that point, another spreadsheet may not be enough.

What is needed is an **independent strategic cross-check**.
